# Glossary

Surf domain, oceanography, and code terms used in the codebase. Skim past anything you already know; the list will shrink over time as concepts stick.

## Surf / oceanography

- **Significant wave height (Hs)** — the average height of the largest 1/3 of waves over a sample period. What NDBC buoys report. The actual face height at a break is typically ~1.5–2× Hs depending on bathymetry. Computed as `Hs = 4·√σ²` where σ² is total spectral variance.
- **Spectral variance density (S(f))** — energy per Hz at each frequency bin in a wave spectrum, in m²/Hz. The "shape of the sea" — tells you which periods are dominant. NDBC publishes 47 bins from 0.0325 Hz (30.8s) to 0.485 Hz (2.06s) in `.data_spec` files.
- **Swell partition / swell train** — a single coherent peak in the wave spectrum (e.g. a 17s SW pulse from a distant storm). The ocean carries multiple partitions simultaneously. Each has its own height, period, and direction. Surfline's "Individual Swells" panel and our SpectralPanel both show this decomposition.
- **Peak-finding decomposition** — algorithm to extract partitions from a spectrum: find local energy maxima, integrate variance in a window around each, compute Hs per partition. Our `decomposeSwellTrains` does this.
- **Energy period (Tₑ)** — `m_{-1}/m₀` where `m_n = ∫f^n·S(f) df`. The period at which most wave energy resides; differs from "peak period" (the period with maximum energy density). Used in the wave-power formula.
- **Wave energy flux** — `P = ρ·g²·m₀·Tₑ / (16π)` in W/m of wave crest. The rate at which wave energy passes through a vertical line in the ocean. Surfers see this as "how much water is moving" — a relative magnitude across all swell trains combined.
- **Dominant period** — time in seconds between successive wave crests. Long-period (15s+) groundswell carries more energy and breaks bigger; short-period (8–10s) wind swell is choppier and breaks smaller for the same Hs.
- **Groundswell** — long-period swell (12s+) generated by distant storms. Travels long distances, organized, surfable.
- **Wind swell** — short-period swell (8–11s) generated by local winds. Less organized, often "junk" but still rideable.
- **Offshore wind** — wind blowing from land toward the ocean. Grooms the wave face; ideal. Each spot's offshore direction depends on its coastal orientation.
- **Onshore wind** — opposite of offshore. Blown out, mushy.
- **Swell window** — the range of compass directions a spot can receive swell from. Spots tucked into coves have narrow windows; open beaches have wide ones.
- **Optimal swell direction** — within a spot's window, the angle that produces the cleanest break shape.
- **Shadow factor (in this codebase)** — 0–1 number describing how much open-ocean swell energy reaches the break from its optimal direction. 1 = fully exposed open coast. 0 = totally blocked. Bolinas spots sit around 0.45–0.55 because Duxbury Reef shelters them.
- **Sand mobility (in this codebase)** — 0–1 number describing how much the bottom contour shifts week-to-week. 0 = pure rock reef (Mavericks, Patch). 1 = constantly-reshaping rivermouth bar (Russian Rivermouth).
- **Rivermouth break** — wave that breaks over a sandbar deposited by a river. Highly variable shape because the bar reshapes with every storm cycle.
- **Reef break** — wave breaking over rock or coral. Stable shape; same place every swell.
- **Beachbreak** — wave breaking over sand. Shape depends on current sandbar configuration.
- **Point break** — wave wrapping around a headland and peeling along the coast.
- **Cove break** — beach or reef tucked into an inlet, protected from prevailing wind/swell.
- **Tow-in surfing** — using a jet ski to tow into waves too big to paddle into. Mavericks gets towed in on contest-grade days.

## Spot / locations

- **The Boneyard** — house-sized boulder field at the inside of Mavericks. Where wipeouts go to be punished.
- **Sea Drift / The Groin** — Bolinas Lagoon mouth break, named for the protective groin structure.
- **Lands End / Sutro Baths** — northwest tip of San Francisco, home to Deadmans (the reef break, not the spot Sigward's webcam shows).
- **Pillar Point Harbor** — north of Half Moon Bay; the harbor breakwater is what `princeton` (Princeton Jetty) sits at. Also the launching point for boats/PWCs going to Mavericks.
- **Salt Point** — northern end of our coverage (Sonoma County). Secrets is at Horseshoe Cove, just north of the state park.

## Code / data shapes

- **`Spot`** — TypeScript interface in `src/lib/data.ts`. The single source of truth for everything we know about a spot: location, optimal conditions, hazards, drive info.
- **`ForecastHour`** — one hour's worth of forecast data for one spot. As of the May 11 pass, carries three separate sets of swell numbers: `swellHeight/Period/Direction` (primary groundswell only, after `shadowFactor` applied), `windWaveHeight/Period/Direction` (local wind-generated), and `combinedHeight/Period/Direction` (groundswell + wind wave total). Plus wind, tide, computed score.
- **`MergedHour`** — server-side equivalent before scoring + `shadowFactor`; the raw merged data from buoy + tide + marine forecast.
- **`SwellTrain`** — one partition extracted from the NDBC `.data_spec` energy spectrum: `{ height, period, direction? }`. Trains arrive sorted longest-period first so `trains[0]` is the cleanest groundswell candidate. Displayed in the SpectralPanel on Spot Detail.
- **`SpectralObservation`** — server-side type returned by `fetchSpectral(buoyId)`: timestamp + trains + energyKwPerM. Merged into `BuoyObservation` in `buildConditions`.
- **`SpecialRule`** — discriminated-union type for per-spot scoring quirks. Currently only `falling-tide-rip` (lagoon outflow turns the wave dangerous on falling tide).
- **`watchOnly`** flag — marks Mavericks as a spectator advisory rather than a "should I surf?" recommendation. Triggers a different `computeScore` path and a different rating scale.
- **`scoreColor(score)`** — 0–100 input → color. Used for rating badges, score numbers, hourly heatmap cells, sparklines.
- **`qualityColor(q)`** — 0–1 input → color. Used for per-metric quality dots, Why-this-score bars, ForecastChart bar fills, compass rose arrows + wedges. Same thresholds as `scoreColor`.
- **`metricQuality(spot, hour, metric)`** — returns 0–1 quality of one specific metric (swellHeight, swellPeriod, windSpeed, tideHeight) for one hour at one spot.
- **`angleDelta(a, b)`** — shortest angular distance between two compass bearings in degrees. Returns 0–180.
- **`degToCardinal(deg)`** — degrees → 16-point compass label (N, NNE, NE, ...).

## App architecture / web

- **PWA (Progressive Web App)** — a website that installs to a phone home screen, runs offline-capable, looks like a native app. Surf Vikings is built as one.
- **Service worker** — background script that caches assets and handles offline behavior. Set up by `vite-plugin-pwa`.
- **Stale-while-revalidate** — caching pattern where the UI immediately renders cached data even if past freshness, then revalidates in the background and updates when fresh data arrives. Used by `useConditions`.
- **Hot Module Replacement (HMR / Fast Refresh)** — Vite feature that updates components in-place without losing state. Useful in dev; occasionally produces ghost error stacks during multi-step edits.
- **`useResponsiveWidth`** — custom hook that wraps a `<div ref>` + `ResizeObserver` so SVG charts can size to their container instead of using a hard-coded width.
- **`useDriveTimes`** — custom hook backed by an OSRM driving matrix; returns `(spot) => minutes` for the current home base.
- **`useConditions`** — custom hook fetching `/api/conditions` with stale-while-revalidate. Returns `{ timelines, response, loading, error, stale }`.

## Data sources

- **NDBC** — National Data Buoy Center, the NOAA agency operating the offshore wave-measurement buoys. Stations referenced: 46013 (Bodega Bay), 46026 (SF Bar Approach), 46237 (SF Bar Entrance / Muir Beach), 46012 (HMB / Buoy 13), 46042 (Monterey Bay).
- **CO-OPS** — Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services; the NOAA arm running tide stations. Stations referenced: 9415020 (Bodega Bay), 9414958 (Bolinas Lagoon), 9414290 (Presidio / SF), 9414131 (Pillar Point), 9413745 (Santa Cruz), 9413450 (Monterey).
- **NWS marine zone** — coastal forecast region used by the National Weather Service. Each spot maps to one (PZZ540, PZZ545, PZZ560, PZZ565). We don't fetch text forecasts but the zone is logged for future use.
- **Open-Meteo** — free European weather/marine API. No key required. We use the `marine` and `forecast` endpoints.
- **OSRM** — Open Source Routing Machine. Public demo server gives free routing without keys.
- **Nominatim** — OpenStreetMap's free geocoder.
- **CUDEM** — Continuously Updated Digital Elevation Model. NOAA NCEI's bathymetry data product. Backlog item: sample these for real per-spot depth profiles.

## Scoring / quality

- **Optimal window** — the contiguous run of forecast hours where score crosses a threshold. Surfaced in the GO NOW box on the dashboard hero card and the OPTIMAL WINDOW box on SpotDetail.
- **Quality** — per-metric 0–1 score of how well a current condition matches a spot's optimal for that metric. Distinct from total score.
- **Score** — total 0–100 weighted sum across all metrics. What appears in the big number on the hero card and SpotDetail header.
- **Rating** — short label associated with a score band: EPIC (80+), GOOD (60–79), FAIR (50–59), MID (35–49), MEH (30–34), POOR (15–29), FLAT (0–14).

## Mavericks-specific

- **Holding period** — Nov 1 to Mar 31, the window during which the contest can be called.
- **Green light** — the contest committee's go-ahead call when conditions meet criteria. Has happened ~10–12 times in 25+ years.
- **Contest criteria** (historical, what the panel uses):
  - Watch tier: Hs ≥ 15ft, period ≥ 15s, direction within ±35° of 305°
  - Firing tier: Hs ≥ 22ft, period ≥ 17s, direction within ±25° of 305°
- **Buoy 46059** — far-offshore California buoy historically used by the contest committee for the 20ft+ Hs criterion. Not in our current mapping but worth knowing about.

## Brand / project terms

- **Eliel** — the user. The app is built for him specifically.
- **Helios** — separate project Eliel runs; pattern source for the planned push-notifications architecture (split logged-out vs logged-in Settings).
- **Set-and-forget** — UX principle: configure once, never look at it again. Applies to home base, favorites, units. Discovery interfaces are explicitly out.
